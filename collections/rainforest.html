Rainforest · Tap & Shower Head

Water, the way
a forest gives it.

Rainforest is our fittings collection — brass, chromed, and drawn down to two shapes: a flat lever that falls under the hand, and a fluted collar that gives the grip its texture.

Three basin mixers and two shower sets. Whichever height the basin asks for, the tap above it belongs to the same family — and the same water, taken overhead, arrives as rain.

  • CP brass
  • Fluted collar
  • Flat lever
  • Ceramic cartridge
  • Ø258 rain head
  • 38 °C thermostatic

One handle, one collar, one family — three heights of water.

The detail

A collar you can feel.

Every mixer in the collection is cast in brass and chrome-plated, and every one carries the same two moves: a flat oval lever that sits low over the body, and a fluted collar turned into the cylinder beneath it.

The fluting is not decoration. It is the part of the tap a wet hand actually meets — fine vertical reeding gives grip where a polished cylinder gives none, and it hides the water marks that a mirror finish shows.

  • i. CP brass, not alloy

    A solid brass body under the chrome — heavier in the hand, and it takes a re-plate decades from now if it ever needs one.

  • ii. Ceramic cartridge

    The lever moves against a ceramic disc, so it stays the same weight to turn on the day it is installed and years after.

  • iii. Three heights, one face

    Under-counter, vessel, or a three-hole spread — specify by the basin, and the detailing stays identical across the room.

The shower sets

Two ways to stand in the rain.

A basin mixer is a thing you touch. A shower is a thing you stand inside, and the difference shows in how the two are specified — a tap is chosen for the counter it sits on, a shower for the wall it has to work with and the water pressure behind it. Both of these are exposed columns: everything on the face of the tile, nothing buried.

SH61028

Shelf Column Shower Set

An exposed column for a wall that already has its plumbing where it is. The riser carries a Ø258 mm overhead out over the standing area, and everything you touch sits together on one shelf at chest height — two handles, and a ledge wide enough for what you actually keep in a shower.

Matt black through the whole set, heads included, so the column reads as one drawn line against the tile rather than as an assembly of parts.

SH8148301

Thermostatic Shower Column

A thermostatic column. You set the temperature once on the right-hand dial and the valve holds it there, whatever the supply does behind the wall — someone running a tap elsewhere in the house stops being your problem. 38 °C is the safety stop; going past it takes a deliberate press.

Three spray patterns, and the button that changes them sits on the head itself rather than down at the valve, so you switch with the hand that is already up there. The shelf runs 400 mm with the flow selectors set into its front edge, close enough to reach without stepping out of the water.
